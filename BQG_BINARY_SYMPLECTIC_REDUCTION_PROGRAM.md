# BQG binary symplectic structure and physical reduction

Date: 2026-11-28  
Status: **derived finite-dimensional kinematic framework; BQG microscopic pullback and nontrivial physical sector remain open.**

This note imports the strongest methodological lesson from null-boundary quantization: derive the symplectic structure on reduced data before declaring canonical commutators. It does not import null cones, their geometric boundary variables, or any result claimed in arXiv:2611.34924.

Proposed BQG route:

\[
\text{binary graph amplitudes}
\to \mathbb P(\mathcal H_{\rm kin})
\to \Omega_{\rm FS}
\to \text{microscopic constraint surface}
\to \text{physical reduction}
\to \text{effective canonical pairs}.
\]

The first two arrows are exact kinematics for any finite Hilbert space. The decisive BQG question is whether the actual binary graph operators select a nontrivial, regulator-stable submanifold and physical reduction.

## 1. A hard obstruction: a set of bits has no symplectic form by itself

For a finite set of binary configurations \(\mathcal B_N=\{0,1\}^N\), the configuration space is discrete. Its tangent spaces are zero-dimensional. Consequently, no nondegenerate differential two-form can be constructed on \(\mathcal B_N\) alone.

A symplectic structure requires continuous directions. In a quantum theory those directions can arise from complex amplitudes and relative phases, not from bit labels alone.

For a fixed regulated graph let

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm kin}^{(\epsilon)}
=\operatorname{span}\{|b\rangle:b\in\mathcal B_\epsilon\},
\]

or, where the BQG Peter--Weyl carrier is larger, use the actual finite Peter--Weyl habitat rather than replacing it by a binary toy space. Normalized pure states modulo overall phase form

\[
\mathbb P(\mathcal H_{\rm kin}^{(\epsilon)})
\simeq \mathbb{CP}^{D_\epsilon-1}.
\]

This projective space has a canonical Fubini--Study symplectic form. It is a kinematic construction, not yet a dynamical consequence of the binary rewrite rules.

## 2. Exact projective symplectic form

Let \(|\psi(\lambda)\rangle\) be a normalized differentiable family of kinematic states with local real parameters \(\lambda^i\). Define \(P_\psi=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\). The pullback of the Fubini--Study form is

\[
\boxed{
(\Omega_{\rm FS})_{ij}
=2\hbar\,\operatorname{Im}
\langle\partial_i\psi|
(1-P_\psi)
|\partial_j\psi\rangle .
}
\]

The projector removes the unphysical overall-phase direction. The form is real and antisymmetric. It is nondegenerate precisely when the chosen projective-state family has no remaining null tangent directions.

For a qubit,

\[
|\psi(\theta,\phi)\rangle
=\cos\frac\theta2|0\rangle
+e^{i\phi}\sin\frac\theta2|1\rangle,
\]

and, with this convention,

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_{\rm FS}
=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\phi .
}
\]

Thus a binary quantum carrier can support continuous symplectic geometry through superposition amplitudes and relative phase. The two basis states by themselves cannot.

For a general affine chart of \(\mathbb{CP}^{D-1}\), write

\[
|\psi(z)\rangle=
\frac{|0\rangle+\sum_{a=1}^{D-1}z^a|a\rangle}
{\sqrt{1+\bar z\cdot z}}.
\]

A Kähler potential is \(K=\hbar\log(1+\bar z\cdot z)\), and

\[
\Omega_{\rm FS}=i\,\partial\bar\partial K.
\]

This gives an exact finite-dimensional candidate two-form without fitting coefficients.

## 3. Pullback must be generated by BQG data, not arbitrary coordinates

The research target is a map

\[
\Phi_\epsilon:\mathcal M_\epsilon
\longrightarrow
\mathbb P(\mathcal H_{\rm kin}^{(\epsilon)}),
\]

where \(\mathcal M_\epsilon\) is built from the actual regulated binary graph variables, amplitudes, and admissible local moves of BQG.

The candidate microscopic two-form is

\[
\boxed{\omega_\epsilon=\Phi_\epsilon^*\Omega_{\rm FS}.}
\]

Its components are computed from actual state derivatives:

\[
(\omega_\epsilon)_{ij}
=2\hbar\,\operatorname{Im}
\langle\partial_i\Phi_\epsilon|
(1-P_{\Phi_\epsilon})
|\partial_j\Phi_\epsilon\rangle.
\]

Required checks:

1. the state map is normalized and differentiable on each declared chart;
2. tangent vectors are computed from frozen BQG update/observable definitions;
3. overall phase and gauge directions are identified independently;
4. the rank of \(\omega_\epsilon\) is computed exactly where possible, otherwise with certified singular-value intervals;
5. rank and induced observables are stable under graph refinement and cutoff changes.

A coordinate chart selected after inspecting the desired answer is not a derivation. Binary transition amplitudes and the state map must be frozen first.

## 4. Constraints and the exact finite physical sector

At fixed regulator, take the declared regulated constraints \(C_A^{(\epsilon)}\) on the same Hilbert space and a positive-definite constraint-label metric \(G\). Define

\[
\mathbb M_\epsilon
=\sum_{A,B}
(C_A^{(\epsilon)})^\dagger
G^{AB}C_B^{(\epsilon)}\geq0.
\]

The exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
\ker\mathbb M_\epsilon
=\bigcap_A\ker C_A^{(\epsilon)}.
}
\]

Therefore the finite physical pure-state set is

\[
\mathcal P_{\rm phys}^{(\epsilon)}
=\mathbb P(\ker\mathbb M_\epsilon).
\]

If \(r_\epsilon=\dim\ker\mathbb M_\epsilon>0\), this set is \(\mathbb{CP}^{r_\epsilon-1}\) and carries the restricted Fubini--Study form. If \(r_\epsilon=1\), it is a point and has no nontrivial symplectic directions. If \(r_\epsilon=0\), there are no physical states at that regulator.

This gives a decisive, fail-closed test: a vacuum vector annihilated by all constraints proves only that the kernel is nonempty; it does not prove a nontrivial physical phase space, propagating degrees of freedom, or a graviton.

The alternative classical route is symplectic reduction of a constraint surface by its first-class gauge flows. It is legal only after the constraint algebra, domains, and gauge distribution have been established. The hypersurface-deformation algebra has phase-space-dependent structure functions; it must not be treated as an ordinary fixed Lie group without proof.

Do not perform a second quotient by gauge orbits on a Hilbert-space kernel if those gauge directions have already been removed by Dirac constraints. Classical Marsden--Weinstein reduction and the quantum common-kernel construction are related routes, not steps to apply blindly in series.

## 5. From the symplectic form to commutators

For a Hermitian operator \(\hat A\), define its expectation-value function

\[
f_A([\psi])=\langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle.
\]

The Fubini--Study Poisson bracket, with the corresponding sign convention, satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\{f_A,f_B\}_{\rm FS}
=\frac{1}{i\hbar}
\langle\psi|[\hat A,\hat B]|\psi\rangle .
}
\]

This is the exact bridge between projective symplectic geometry and the operator commutator algebra. It is not an independent derivation of the operators: it checks that their expectation functions realize the same bracket.

If BQG reduction yields local Darboux coordinates \((q^a,p_a)\), then

\[
\omega_{\rm phys}
=\sum_a dq^a\wedge dp_a,
\qquad
\{q^a,p_b\}_{\rm phys}=\delta^a_b.
\]

The corresponding quantum target is

\[
[\hat q^a,\hat p_b]
=i\hbar\delta^a_b
\]

on the appropriate physical domain, or the corresponding Weyl relations.

**Finite-dimensional warning.** No finite-dimensional matrices can satisfy \([\hat q,\hat p]=i\hbar I\) exactly on the entire finite Hilbert space, since the trace of a commutator is zero while \(\operatorname{tr}(i\hbar I)\neq0\). At finite cutoff the correct tests are projected/local commutator estimates, controlled leakage, and convergence toward the canonical/Weyl algebra in a declared large-cutoff or semiclassical regime. A global exact canonical pair must not be claimed from a finite matrix fit.

The qubit example is compact: \(\mathbb{CP}^1\) is a sphere and has no global Darboux chart. Canonical coordinates exist only locally. This is why physical BQG variables must be derived, not assigned by relabeling binary bits as \(q,p\).

## 7. Required numerical/algebraic gate

Implement one frozen regulator and one frozen state chart before scaling up. For every chart point, store:

- regulator, graph, and basis hash;
- state-vector normalization residual;
- tangent-vector construction and derivative check;
- Fubini--Study two-form matrix \(\Omega_{ij}\);
- rank of \(\Omega\), null vectors, and singular-value intervals;
- constraint residuals \(\|C_A\psi\|\);
- master value \(\langle\psi|\mathbb M|\psi\rangle\);
- dimension of the exact or numerically certified common kernel;
- pullback rank after gauge directions are removed;
- commutator-versus-Poisson residuals for a frozen observable set;
- refinement and cutoff comparison.

Fail-closed criteria:

- rank of \(\Omega\) must be even;
- its nullspace must match only independently identified phase, gauge, or redundant directions;
- the physical common kernel must contain more than the vacuum alone to support nontrivial physical dynamics;
- the induced physical two-form must be nondegenerate after reduction;
- commutator/Poisson agreement must improve under the declared limit, not just at one cutoff;
- no fitted normalization may be chosen to force the canonical bracket.

## 8. First concrete BQG calculation

Do not start with the full graph or all Lorentzian sectors. Start with the smallest existing binary carrier for which the repository already has exact state vectors and constraint actions.

1. Freeze the smallest nontrivial binary/Walsh carrier and its orthonormal basis.
2. Build the projective state family from its actual complex amplitudes, retaining relative phases.
3. Compute \(\Omega_{\rm FS}\) exactly for a generic chart point.
4. Restrict actual BQG constraint matrices to that carrier; compute the exact common kernel and its dimension.
5. Compute the restricted symplectic form on the physical projective kernel.
6. Use already-defined Hermitian BQG observables, not fitted matrices, and compare their Fubini--Study Poisson brackets with operator commutators.
7. Repeat on the next graph/refinement and check stability.

If the smallest carrier has only a one-dimensional common kernel, record a valid no-go for that carrier and move to the next one. Do not weaken the constraint set to manufacture a phase space.

## 9. What this would and would not close

A successful calculation would establish, for the tested finite BQG carrier:

- a symplectic form induced by its actual quantum amplitudes;
- the dimension and nondegeneracy of its physical reduced state manifold;
- a verified correspondence between expectation-value Poisson brackets and BQG commutators;
- candidate local canonical coordinates, with the finite-cutoff limitation stated above.

It would not by itself establish the full Lorentzian constraint algebra/HDA, a nonzero continuum rigging map, two propagating massless TT graviton polarizations, the Einstein-Hilbert infrared effective action, a universal Lorentzian light cone, or black-hole entropy from BQG microscopic dynamics. Those remain separate physical gates.
